# Read aloud to an adult, measure separately

*Provenance: research ran on Claude Opus 5.5 because the account's Fable limit was hit; numbers are drawn from research notes kept outside this repo, with many sources seen at abstract level only. Seven high-impact claims were rechecked against sources before filing (see the end).*

## Conclusion

What most plausibly improves L's fluency is short, daily reading of connected text aloud to an adult who models and gives feedback, with decoding practice aimed only at the words and patterns she actually stumbles on; no method has strong evidence at exactly this age, none has been tested in Lithuanian, and a realistic pooled effect is about 0.3 to 0.5 standard deviations rather than the larger figures often quoted. The home routine should therefore be about 7 minutes with a parent on most days, using a new text most days with overlapping words and up to three varied passes in one sitting, which respects her dislike of repeated texts without losing the support that makes rereading work; this schedule is our inference, not a tested programme. The tablet should stop acting as a speedometer, because a tap per word alone proves nothing about reading: it should supply a model, phrase-cued text and light word practice, and it should give up some of its syllable and quiz time, since syllable training improves trained items far more than text speed. L's true oral speed is unknown, because no human has ever timed her reading, so the first job is a stopwatched baseline in the autumn break of 2 to 8 November 2026. No public standardised Lithuanian grade 2 norm was found, and although her tap figure looks very low against the weak references available, it is not a valid oral rate and should not be compared with them. On this evidence, b/d confusion alone is not a cause for concern at 7, and speech recognition, forced pacing and cloud audio should not be built now. Parent help is promising but less proven than reviews suggest, and parent scoring is unvalidated, so the numbers are for watching trends over 8 to 12 weeks and not for weekly judgements.

## Recommended weekly routine

All minutes, days and text lengths below are the author's proposal. The trials closest to this used about 15 minutes three times a week, about 45 minutes in total. Whether short daily sessions work as well is untested.

| Day | Parent with L | App alone | Measurement | Speed data? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon to Fri | 7 min: one new short text, up to three passes (below) | 5 to 8 min: phrase model, words from the coming text, comprehension | None | **None. The app-alone part produces no speed data** |
| Sat | Measure first, before any other reading. Then an optional 7 min session of her choice | Optional | Week A: three passages, then one word list (about 4 min reading). Week B: real-word and pseudoword lists (2 min reading) | Yes, parent-timed from audio only |
| Sun | Free. Reading to her for pleasure is welcome but does not count as fluency practice | Optional | None | None |

**The 7-minute session**
1. L picks one of two new texts. Keep texts at about 20 to 40 words for now and adjust after baseline. A 48-word text at 12 wpm would take 4 minutes per pass.
2. Pass 1, listen or echo: the parent reads a phrase, L echoes it.
3. Pass 2, solo aloud: L reads to the parent. If she is stuck, wait about 3 seconds, then give the word. Afterwards, reread the one or two words she stuck on. The notes found no good comparison of correction methods, so keep this simple and pleasant.
4. Pass 3, perform: L reads to a toy, a sibling or the other parent, in a voice she picks. If she resists, two passes are enough, though that is below the tested range.
5. Praise something specific ("you worked out that long word"). Do not pay stars for merely finishing.

**Texts.** Write them as a series with recurring characters and a shared word pool, so the words repeat while the story is new. Prefer exact word forms over shared stems. With a parent, about 90% accuracy is acceptable. Texts for alone use should be easier. These rules are inference from the sources in question 4.

**What the app does.** It plays a phrase model, shows phrase-cued text, and practises chunks taken from the coming text. It never reports wpm from an alone read.

**What the parent does.** The parent listens, models and gives light feedback, runs the Saturday measure, and scores from audio later without L present (the author estimates 15 to 20 minutes for a passage week). About an hour of practice scoring comes first.

**b/d, once in the first week.** Run three checks: untimed b versus d identification, the count of errors in each direction, and whether b/d errors appear in connected reading. After that, use 1 to 2 minutes of handwriting inside real words, twice a week, only if errors appear in connected reading. This dose is the author's judgement.

### Measurement protocol

CBM (curriculum-based measurement) is a short timed read repeated over time to show a trend. No validated Lithuanian version was found. This protocol adapts English rules and is a proposal throughout.

**A weekly measure was requested, but the notes recommend something different.** They recommend three passages every two weeks, with word lists in the other weeks, because a single weekly passage is too noisy to judge. To give a number every week, we add one real-word list every Saturday (the author's extension). The list gives the weekly number. The passages give the decision number. Do not decide anything from one week.

**Schedule**
- **Baseline: three sittings in the autumn break (2 to 8 Nov 2026), for example Tue 3, Thu 5 and Sat 7 Nov.** Each has three passages. The break is a possible easy time, but holiday conditions may differ from term. The parent read already planned for Sat 7 Nov can be one of these if the materials are ready. If they are not, start the baseline when they are, and move the review dates to match.
- Add the official NŠA one-minute word list ([NŠA toolkit archive](https://emokykla.lt/upload/files/2023/07/19/diagnostiniai-skaitymo-vertinimo-irankiai-1-3-klasei.7z)), given as written, once at baseline and once at week 12. Using it only twice limits practice effects on a fixed list (the author's inference).
- In the same week, have her read one matched passage in the app, with a parent present, to calibrate tap speed against oral speed. Old tap figures are not comparable with anything until this is done.
- Then: passage Saturdays on 21 Nov, 5 Dec, 19 Dec, 2 Jan, 16 Jan and 30 Jan, with list-only Saturdays between them.

**Materials**
- At least 24 new passages of 100 or more words each, so she never reaches the end. Her current 20 to 55 word texts cannot be used, because they cap the score.
- One story per passage, large font, no pictures, digits or abbreviations, few proper names, never used in training.
- Match passages on syllables per word, frequent words and sentence length. Passage order is fixed in advance at random.
- At least 12 real-word list forms (1 to 4 syllable words, shuffled) and 6 pseudoword forms, none used in training. The notes specify six real-word forms; twelve are needed for weekly use.

**Steps for a passage**
1. Same day, time, room and parent where possible. Skip if she is ill, upset or very tired, and log the miss.
2. Start a phone recording and keep it out of her sight. Show no timer and no number.
3. Say (proposed Lithuanian wording, translated from DIBELS, a US measure): "Perskaityk šitą tekstą garsiai. Jei užstrigsi, pasakysiu žodį ir skaitysi toliau. Skaityk kaip gali geriausiai." Never say "greitai" (fast).
4. Time starts at her first word on the audio. The title does not count. Stay silent except under the hesitation rule.
5. Let her finish her sentence after about a minute. Scoring stops at 60.0 seconds on the audio.
6. Ask one easy question about what she read and record 0 or 1. This is not part of any score. Move on without giving a number. Stop for the day if she is distressed.

**Error rules** (S substitution, O omission, E wrong ending, H word supplied by the parent)
- Misread, omitted or out-of-order words are errors. Each repeat misreading counts.
- **Hesitation (proposal):** after 3 seconds of silence, supply the word; it is an error. Audible sounding-out continues for up to 8 seconds, then supply the word; it is an error. The standard 3-second limit would mark most words wrong at about 5 seconds per word. Review this rule at about 40 wpm and log the date of any change.
- Sounded out and blended is correct. Sounded out but never blended is an error.
- A self-correction before she moves past the next word is correct. Insertions and correct repetitions are ignored.
- **Lithuanian rule (proposal, not evidence):** an ending that produces a different written form is an error (E), even if the sentence still makes sense, for example *mergaitė* read as *mergaitę*. Consistent family or regional pronunciation is not an error, and neither is wrong stress on otherwise correct sounds. Agree the list of family pronunciations beforehand and do not change the rules mid-series.

**Scoring, per passage**
- **WCPM** (words correct per minute) = words attempted in 60 s minus errors.
- **CSPM** (correct syllables per minute) = syllables in the words read correctly. CSPM is the headline number, because it absorbs some of the difference in word length. There is precedent in Italian and German research, but no head-to-head validity test.
- Compute strict (E is wrong) and lenient (E is right). If the gap grows, she may be guessing endings.
- Chart the median of the three passages and keep all three scores.
- **Accuracy gate, pooled over three passages (borrowed from an English rule of thumb):** 93% or more means the rate is interpretable. At 90 to 92%, look at the passages. Below 90% in two consecutive sessions, check text difficulty and decoding rather than speed.
- **Word lists:** items correct in 60 s, at most one error per word, self-corrections are not errors. Use the rate only, because errors are unreliable from session to session.

**Record:** date, time, passage or form ID, syllables in the passage, words attempted, errors by code, words correct, correct syllables, plus mood, interruptions and prompts given. Log b/d misreads separately.

**Scorer check.** Score from audio, not live. A second listen or the other parent rescores one session in four without seeing the first score, aiming to agree within 1 word per passage. Keep recordings on the phone, private, and out of the repository. Delete them after the 12-week review unless they are needed for a conversation with a professional (the author's proposal).

**Expected progress (proposal, no Lithuanian growth data exists).** Re-anchor after baseline. The wpm column assumes 2.5 syllables per word.

| Gain per week, CSPM | 12 weeks, CSPM | 12 weeks, wpm | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under 0.5 | Under +6 | Under +2 to 3 | Cannot be told from no growth |
| 0.5 to 1 | +6 to +12 | +2 to +5 | Modest, keep watching |
| **1 to 2.5** | **+12 to +30** | **+5 to +12** | **Sensible expectation** |
| Over 2.5 | Over +30 | Over +12 | Ambitious |

Expect one occasion to wobble by about 2 to 4 wpm at her likely level. That band is extrapolated from English data at much higher speeds, so replace it with the spread of her own three passages. Do not show her the line, because noise guarantees apparent falls.

**Review points**
- **Any single session or two-session change:** ignore.
- **About week 8 (around 2 Jan 2027, five occasions):** change one thing only if the signal is obvious, meaning a slope clearly near zero or accuracy below the gate. Mark the change on the chart. Precision is only about plus or minus 0.7 wpm per week (derived, assuming 3 wpm error).
- **About week 12 (around 30 Jan 2027, seven occasions):** below +6 CSPM with practice actually done, first check dose, text difficulty, passage drift and scorer consistency, then change the plan substantially. +6 to +12: continue with an adjustment. +12 or more: continue.

**Worth asking a professional (the class teacher or school specialist first).** This list was not verified against authoritative guidance, because the search for referral criteria was not completed. No data threshold is needed before asking:
- a parent or teacher is worried, or L shows distress or avoidance around reading;
- errors on many letters or sounds, beyond mirror pairs;
- b/d errors that persist in untimed connected reading;
- accuracy under 90% on easy texts for three or more sessions;
- growth under about 0.5 CSPM per week after a properly delivered 12-week block, or flat lists and text together;
- any concern about hearing or sight, which is a separate check to do first.

A single timed flash result is not on this list.

## Evidence

Strength labels come from the research notes: **Strong** means several pooled randomised trials at a relevant age, **Moderate** means pooled or randomised evidence with real limits, **Low** means small trials or indirect evidence, and **Very low** means reviews find no credible benefit. Effect sizes in standard deviations (SD, d or g) compare with a control group. They do not mean reaching age level. Unless stated otherwise, samples are English-speaking.

### 1. What has the strongest evidence, and what is weak or absent

**Reading aloud to an adult**
- A grade 2 randomised trial used trained adult listeners, 15 minutes three times a week for 20 weeks. It favoured reading aloud on all four fluency measures, improvement index 29 to 41 ([O'Connor 2010, WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/study/82978)). Moderate, single study; WWC page read.
- Guided oral reading: weighted effect 0.41, fluency 0.44, poor readers 0.33, grades 2 to 9 ([NRP Chapter 3](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/publications/pubs/nrp/Documents/ch3.pdf)). Moderate; relevant passages read.
- Daily connected text is rated Moderate and decoding work Strong for K to 3 ([Foorman 2016 practice guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/21)). Ratings page read only.

**Repeated reading, the most-cited base**
- A quality review found only one high-quality study among 11, and concluded it was not evidence-based by those criteria, while still seeing a likely positive effect ([Chard 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/001440290907500301)). High relevance as a caution; abstract only.
- Gains on practised passages are moderate to large, and transfer is only minor to moderate ([Strickland 2013](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-e614-655ce4dd454a887da61dfdde8f5fd88b9d79d3bb56e3688ffc835278f4c307eb3)). Moderate; abstract only.
- The synthesis favouring repeated reading rests mostly on single-subject studies ([Stevens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5097019)). Moderate; read.
- Treat g = 1.41 ([Lee & Yoon](https://doi.org/10.1177/0022219415605194)) as too high for planning. Abstract only.

**Phonics and word-level work**
- Word reading fluency SMD 0.45, English only ([McArthur 2018, Cochrane](https://www.cochrane.org/hr/evidence/CD009115_phonics-training-english-speaking-poor-readers)). Moderate; summary page read.
- Phonics g' = 0.32; fluency training g' = 0.30 with a wide interval, which is under-powered rather than null ([Galuschka 2014](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/33740/1/10.1371_journal.pone.0089900.pdf)). Moderate for this use; read.
- Phonics fades from .29 to .07 at follow-up in younger samples; fluency interventions from .47 to .28 ([Suggate 2016](https://epub.uni-regensburg.de/35639/1/0022219414528540.pdf)). Strong for durability; read.
- Intensive K to 3 intervention: 0.39, adjusted to 0.28 for publication bias ([Wanzek 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6247899)). Moderate to strong, but not parent-delivered; read.

**Syllable training: strong on items, weak on text**
- Finnish grades 2 to 3 (mean age 9), randomised, n = 150: gains on trained syllables, word transfer only for long infrequent ones, no transfer to text with few practised syllables ([Heikkilä 2013](https://jyx.jyu.fi/bitstream/handle/123456789/42638/heikkilaetalsyllablerecognitionfinaldraft.pdf?sequence=1)). Moderate; accepted manuscript read.
- Finnish grades 4 to 6, n = 36: no text-level gain ([Huemer 2010](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/repeated-reading-of-syllables-among-finnish-speaking-children-wit/)). Low to moderate; abstract only.
- German grade 4: word fluency 0.82, on a measure with test-retest of only .25, and text fluency was not measured ([Müller 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5611416/)). Low to moderate; read.
- German grade 2: word recognition and comprehension improved ([Müller 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2020.101304)). Abstract only; effect size not retrieved. It is the most age-relevant trial, and it is a reason to keep some syllable work.
- **Implication:** `syllable_build` alone will not fix text speed. Connected reading to an adult should be the core. Using chunks from the coming text is the author's extrapolation.

**Parents: large in reviews, tempered by a trial**
- Parents listening d = 0.52, skill tutoring d = 1.15, parents reading to the child d = 0.18 ([Sénéchal & Young 2008, cited by Villiger 2020, not read](https://phrepo.phbern.ch/2345/1/Villiger2020.BenefitsandConstraintsofParentInvolvementinChildrensReading.pdf)). Parent tutoring 0.55 in trimmed group studies ([Erion 2006, record](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/parent-tutoring-reading-instruction-and-curricular-assessment)). Low to moderate, largely weak designs.
- Randomised trial, n = 198 in the abstract (the record's metadata says 133), Swiss German-language grade 3: volunteers d = .21 on fluency, not lasting; **parent tutors not significantly better than controls** ([Villiger 2019 record](https://nssa.stanford.edu/studies/effectiveness-extracurricular-program-struggling-readers-comparative-study-parent-tutors-0); [Villiger 2020](https://phrepo.phbern.ch/2345/1/Villiger2020.BenefitsandConstraintsofParentInvolvementinChildrensReading.pdf)). Moderate; abstract and chapter. The record lists an effect of -0.1, which conflicts with the abstract.
- **Honest reading:** one trial, older children, a different language. It does not show that parents cannot help, but do not quote 1.15 as an expectation. The same chapter notes parent help can bring conflict. Short and pleasant sessions are the author's response, not a tested finding.

**Motivation**
- Choice: intrinsic motivation d = 0.30, subsequent learning d = 0.10, not significant ([Patall 2008](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/2008_PatallCooperRobinson_PsychBulletin.pdf)). Strong, not reading-specific; passages read.
- Completion-contingent rewards d = -0.36, positive feedback d = +0.33 ([Deci 1999](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589297/)). Strong but contested, mostly lab tasks; abstract only.

**Weak or absent**
- Coloured overlays: similar to placebo ([Griffiths 2016](https://www.pubmed.gov/27580753/)). Very low; abstract only.
- Dyslexia fonts: no benefit, in dyslexia population samples ([Kuster](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5934461)). Very low; abstract only.
- Vision training: not endorsed ([AAP joint statement](https://aao.org/Assets/c1788b54-c9d1-4f76-86e8-256da8a04841/634965436411730000/learning-disabilities-dyslexia-and-vision-2009-pdf)). A position statement; abstract only.
- Working-memory training: no transfer to decoding ([Melby-Lervåg & Hulme 2013](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8757y/is-working-memory-training-effective-a-meta-analytic-review)). Very low; abstract only.
- Brain Gym: critical reviews only ([Spaulding 2010](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........e02fd7dc61e99f73ecf4ddaf6a47e61b)). Very low; abstract only.
- Silent reading alone for fluency: not shown ([NRP Chapter 3](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/sites/default/files/publications/pubs/nrp/Documents/ch3.pdf)). Low. This is no reason to discourage enjoyable book time.
- Absent: any Lithuanian trial, any sessions under 10 minutes, any study of passage length.

**L's data (live database, read 2026-11-01).** Across 29 sets, the item mix is `syllable_build` 109, `choice` 83, `word_gap` 37, `read_aloud` 30, `number_input` 9, `letter_hunt` 9, `letter_flash` 9, `open_schema` 1. Exposure is mostly word-building and quiz, with little connected oral reading to an adult. Comprehension is about 88 to 90% first try and 93% on a school diagnostic; `syllable_build` is 79%. Good comprehension says little about decoding load.

### 2. Lithuanian and transparent-orthography specifics

A transparent orthography is one where spelling maps regularly to sound. Lithuanian is classed as one ([Labanienė 2020](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:70097438/70097438.pdf)), with fairly complex syllables ([Silinskas et al.](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:171328463/171328463.pdf)).

**No public standardised Lithuanian grade 2 norm was found.**
- A Vilnius University dissertation says directly that Lithuania has no standardised reading-skills tests yet, and points to the NEC reading test for identifying difficulties ([Labanienė 2020](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:70097438/70097438.pdf), p. 119). Read.
- The official NŠA toolkit measures words per minute but gives no expected value, percentile or cut-off in its speed materials ([archive](https://emokykla.lt/upload/files/2023/07/19/diagnostiniai-skaitymo-vertinimo-irankiai-1-3-klasei.7z)). Read.
- One closed regional norm exists (Gedutienė et al. 2008, Klaipėda region). Its values were not seen, and it is described only in Labanienė. It is the one known source that could change this answer.
- **Search limits:** several Lithuanian and English queries were run, plus the toolkit and the dissertation. There were no author-specific searches and no scoping to Lithuanian education journals, and several theses went unopened. So this means "not found", not "does not exist".

**Weak public points. These are not norms.**

| Source | Group | Figure | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| One Lithuanian school's public report, Nov 2023 (read; link held in the private notes, to avoid naming any school in this public repo) | Grade 2, current year / end of grade 2 | 43 / 50 wpm | One school, no sample size, no SD, testing date not given, use of the NŠA list assumed |
| [Labanienė 2020](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:70097438/70097438.pdf) (read) | End of grade 1, N = 92 | Mean 37, SD 23 words correct per minute | Enriched for reading risk by design; grade 1 |
| [Silinskas et al.](https://epublications.vu.lt/object/elaba:171328463/171328463.pdf) (read) | Start of grade 1, n = 183 | 15.61 words in 45 s; about 21 per minute (derived) | Word list, one time point |

**Italian percentiles, in syllables per second** ([Cornoldi, Tressoldi & Perini 2010](https://www.airipa.it/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/NuoveNormeMT010.pdf)). These are the strongest usable comparison. Tables were read through text extraction, and they are a different language and task.

| Time | Mean | 15th | 5th |
|---|---|---|---|
| Grade 1 end | 1.40 | 0.74 | 0.57 |
| Grade 2 start | 1.43 | 0.87 | 0.70 |
| Grade 2 mid | 1.89 | 1.18 | 0.95 |
| Grade 2 end | 2.48 | 1.54 | 1.33 |

Two raw Italian mid-year samples differ by 0.68 syllables per second, which is a warning about any "norm". Spanish grade 2 is 72.96 wpm, with extreme heterogeneity between samples ([Ripoll Salceda 2020](https://ojs.ehu.eus/index.php/psicodidactica/article/download/23345/20701)). Read. English norms ([Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017](https://www.brtprojects.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/TechRpt_1702ORFNorms_Fini.pdf)) do not transfer, because Lithuanian words are likely longer.

**Reject the "90 to 110 wpm state standard".** It appeared only in search excerpts from translated pages, for example [this host](https://lt.unistica.com/kiek-zodziu-per-minute-turetu-skaityti-pirmojo-rengejo/). Which page carries which figure was not confirmed. It looks machine-translated from another country's material. Unverified; do not use.

**The honest message.** At 12 wpm, L's figure converts to 0.40 to 0.58 syllables per second, depending on word length (our conversion). That looks very low against the references above, below the Italian grade 2 start 5th percentile. **But 12 wpm is tap-based and may include tap time, so it is not an oral rate and must be calibrated by a stopwatched read before being compared with anything.** The size of the tap overhead is unknown. Measure first.

**Word length matters**
- Standard Lithuanian text averages about 2.9 syllables per word ([Karosienė & Girdenis 2000](https://baltistica.lt/index.php/baltistica/article/view/488), abstract only). NŠA children's texts average 2.06 to 2.40 (the notes' own approximate count).
- At a constant 0.5 syllables per second, the same reader gets about 15 wpm at 2.0 syllables per word and about 10 wpm at 2.9 (derived). Small wpm changes between texts may be word length alone.
- Texts with fewer rare long words gave greater rate gains in English grade 2 ([Hiebert 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/02702710590930528), passages read). Applying this to Lithuanian is untested.

**What transparent-orthography research adds**
- Most European orthographies reach accurate foundation reading by the end of grade 1; Lithuanian was not studied ([Seymour 2003](https://eclass-b.uoa.gr/modules/document/file.php/PPP363/Seymour_Aro_Erskine.pdf)). Read.
- German: accuracy near ceiling, and 70% of children dysfluent in grade 1 remained poor readers in grade 8 ([Landerl & Wimmer 2008](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/development-of-word-reading-fluency-and-spelling-in-a-consistent-/)). Strong; abstract only. This is a population finding and says nothing about L. It is a reason not to delay asking for input if worried.
- Speed is the lasting differentiator, and rapid naming predicts speed ([Moll 2014](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00965264), abstract only). Rapid naming predicted initial speed but not the training effect in Heikkilä, so it is not a training target.
- So track speed first, keep accuracy as a guard rail, and check accuracy on L's long words rather than assume it.

### 3. b/d confusion at 7

- First, third and fifth graders were all slower on b/d than on non-mirror pairs ([Ahr 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2015.12.009)). Moderate; abstract only.
- Children who later read less well made more letter errors overall but not more reversals, in writing at age 5 to 6 ([Treiman 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/10888438.2013.873937)). Moderate; abstract only. This is the writing literature, not reading at 7.
- Population research only: the link between reversals and dyslexia is described as mixed, and reversals are common in other beginning readers ([Blackburne 2014](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0098386), passages read; [Brooks 2011](https://doi.org/10.1080/87565641.2011.606401), abstract only). This says nothing about L.
- Embedded picture cues reduced confusion and transferred to words, in 5-year-olds learning Hebrew letters ([Shmidman & Ehri 2010](https://doi.org/10.1080/10888430903117492)). Moderate, indirect; abstract only. The English "bed" trick has no evidence found, and "bed" is "lova" in Lithuanian. A cue such as "balionas" for b is an untested illustration.
- Mirror-letter training with air-writing reported doubled fluency in Brazilian first graders ([Torres 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2020.11.031)). Abstract only. Commentators call the b/d result unreplicated ([Fischer & Luxembourger 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fcomm.2021.719652/full), passages read). Do not rely on the doubling claim or copy the 7.5 hours.
- Separating similar items speeds learning, tested with sounds in preschoolers ([Carnine 1976](https://scilit.com/publications/a3e264375df9e31cc67db25c16ce7442), search summary only). Extending it to b/d is untested.
- Isolated word recognition training was the least effective fluency approach across 30 single-subject studies ([Morgan & Sideridis 2006](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-5826.2006.00218.x)). Abstract read per one note; another note could not obtain the findings.
- No normative curve for b/d errors in reading at 7 was found, and no study validates a timed flash as a letter-knowledge test.

**L's data.** `letter_flash` shows a letter for 750 to 1000 ms, it disappears, then two buttons appear. A round passes at accuracy of at least 0.9. So "passes 4 of 7" is a pass mark, not 57% accuracy.
- Over all 13 stored rounds, including replays: 114 trials, 12 errors, 89.5% correct. When b was shown she chose d 7 of 58 times (12%). When d was shown she chose b 5 of 56 times (9%).
- **Over all rounds, errors are close to symmetric. The most recent 8 rounds lean b→d (4 b→d, 1 d→b; 5 errors in 64 trials, 92% correct). That fits the parent's impression, but it is too few errors to call a bias.**
- Two of the lowest rounds had the fastest medians (205 and 208 ms, accuracy 0.8 and 0.75), which suggests some errors are tap speed. Others are not (0.6 at 748 ms). 8 of 13 rounds passed at 0.9 or more; 5 of the last 8 passed.

**Reading:** the flash measures speed under the conditions where b/d is slowest. It is a discrimination signal, not a test of knowledge. Keep it brief and low stakes, stop using it as pass or fail, and decide from the untimed check and connected reading.

### 4. Her dislike of repeated texts versus the repeated-reading evidence

**The tension.** Repeated reading has the most-cited base. But the rigorous part of that base is thin (Chard 2009, above), and transfer to new text is the weak link.

- Grade 2 classrooms: scaffolded reading of different texts matched weekly repeated reading, and its benefits came earlier ([Kuhn 2006](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15548430jlr3804_1)). Moderate; abstract only.
- Follow-up: wide-reading classes had better fluency and self-concept than controls, and repeated-reading pupils were more often off task ([Schwanenflugel 2009](https://doi.org/10.1080/19388070802422415)). Moderate; abstract only.
- Repetitive approaches showed no clear advantage, and assisted beat unassisted ([Kuhn & Stahl 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037//0022-0663.95.1.3)). Review; abstract only.
- Grade 3: either method used exclusively tended toward tedium ([Reutzel 2008](https://doi.org/10.3200/joer.102.1.37-50)). Abstract only.
- **Unsupported** new text alone is modest: d = 0.176, fluency d = 0.105, but no significant difference from repeated reading in direct comparisons ([Zimmermann, Reed & Aloe](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-Meta-Analysis-of-Non-Repetitive-Reading-Fluency/9984371100102771)). Low to moderate, eight studies; abstract only. "Wide" in the trials above means adult-scaffolded, so support cannot be dropped.
- Speed gains depend on shared words between texts ([Rashotte & Torgesen 1985](https://www.colorincolorado.org/node/54285), abstract only). Poorer readers transferred from words alone, grade 4 ([Faulkner & Levy 1999](https://doi.org/10.3758/bf03210817), passages read). Low; older and small. No optimal overlap is established.
- Three readings beat one ([Sindelar 1990](https://doi.org/10.1080/00220671.1990.10885959), abstract only). Six readings add practised-text speed without better generalisation ([Stevens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5097019), read). No study tests two readings as a dose.
- In a transparent language: three Greek grade 2 pupils, large gains on practised texts, only +2 to +4 words per minute on unseen text, percentile unchanged ([Padeliadu 2021](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1295344.pdf)). Very weak design, but directly on point; read.
- Unresolved: O'Connor 2007 favours repeated reading in one account ([Stevens 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5097019)) and shows no difference in another ([PLD summary](https://support.pld-literacy.org/en-au/article/re-reading-vs-continuous-reading-for-fluency-what-does-the-evidence-say-18k20t7/)). The article was not read.

**Recommendation for L: new text most days with overlapping words, and up to three varied passes within one sitting, rather than the same text across days.**

**Reasoning (the author's inference, not a tested schedule).** Overlap is the likely mechanism behind repetition's transfer. Three passes reach the best-evidenced floor. Varying the passes keeps a model and a purpose. Her complaint matches a documented pattern of off-task behaviour and tedium, so it is a design signal and not just a preference. **The cost is unknown:** no study compares within-sitting with across-day repetition at this age. If new-text sessions drop to fewer than three passes or lose the adult, the small unsupported effect is what to expect.

**L's data.** Vėžlys was 6 wpm on day 1, 21 on day 2, 5 on day 3: no clean gain across days, on mixed-quality data. Do not over-read this. A faster reread is not skill growth in any case; the measure is a cold, unseen passage.

### 5. A reliable measure a parent can run

- Standard rules: an unpractised passage, one minute, with errors defined as misread, omitted, out of order, or supplied after 3 seconds ([Hasbrouck & Tindal 2017](https://www.brtprojects.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/TechRpt_1702ORFNorms_Fini.pdf); [DIBELS 8 guide](https://dibels.uoregon.edu/sites/default/files/2026-07/dibels8_admin_scoring_guide.pdf)). Read; English. DIBELS suggests every two weeks for at-risk K to 3 pupils.
- Three passages with median scores is standard at benchmark ([Acadience](https://acadiencelearning.org/learning-materials/oral-reading-fluency/)). Publisher page only.
- One-minute reads have about 10 words of error, likely 5 to 9 under good conditions, in English at much higher speeds ([Christ & Silberglitt 2007](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/estimates-of-the-standard-error-of-measurement-for-curriculum-bas/)). Strong; abstract only.
- Grade 2 error fell from 13.30 with one form to 9.46 with three ([Anderson 2013 slides](https://brtprojects.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/NASP2013_AndersonEtAlV2.pdf)). Weak to moderate; read.
- Six "comparable" passages ranged from 67.9 to 93.9 words per minute in grade 2 ([Francis 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2396583)). Strong design; abstract only. Passage matching therefore matters more than anything else. For a single child, matching can only be approximate.
- Slopes: adequate at about 14 probes with very good data ([Christ 2012](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/curriculum-based-measurement-of-oral-reading-quality-of-progress-/), abstract only). Less-than-weekly collection may be viable ([January 2018](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/progress-monitoring-in-reading-comparison-of-weekly-bimonthly-and/), abstract only). Seven occasions of three passages is not the same as 14 occasions, so week 12 is still a cautious decision.
- Word lists: in German, reading-time reliability was .93 to .95 and error reliability .60 to .65 ([Wimmer 2000](https://plus.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/1787194.pdf)). Strong for this scoring point; read. One-minute lists reach parallel-form reliability of .90 to .98 ([SLRT-II review](https://www.dbl-ev.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/SLRT-II_-_2021.pdf), third-party review read). Lists and text tap partly different skills ([van Viersen 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.789313/pdf), abstract only), so a list cannot replace passages.
- Audio beats live: live examiners made more mismarkings than audio raters (d = 0.59), and scorers disagreed on words correct in 98% of passages ([Reed 2019](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Accuracy-in-identifying-students-miscues-during/9983993336302771)). Moderate, grades 5 to 6; abstract only. Examiners accounted for about 16% of variance ([Cummings 2014](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/Examiner-error-in-curriculum-based-measurement-of/9983993486902771)). Abstract only.
- **Parent scoring is unvalidated.** The closest study reports trained parents scoring accurately, with no comparison to examiners ([Casey & Williamson 2011](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/10723)). Weak; abstract only.
- Timing: hidden versus visible stopwatch and different instructions made no significant difference to scores in grade 3 ([Taylor 2013](https://digitalcommons.memphis.edu/facpubs/8939)). Moderate; abstract only; anxiety not reported. Surveys raise anxiety concerns ([Deeney & Shim 2016](https://digitalcommons.uri.edu/education_facpubs/15)). Weak for causation. A hidden timer costs nothing.
- Growth, English: grade 2 about 1.71 then 1.02 words per week ([Christ 2010](https://www.readnaturally.com/userfiles/ckfiles/files/christ-silberglitt-yeo-cormier.pdf)). Strong; read. **English only, and probably too high for long Lithuanian words.**
- Growth, Italian: a slow-reader sample gained about 0.3 syllables per second per grade ([Tressoldi 2001](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/84469925)). Search excerpt, confirmed in a secondary source. Used only as a growth figure; it implies nothing about L.
- No published rule on wrong inflectional endings was found in the sources reached. DIBELS's in-context criterion points toward counting them as errors, hence the proposal above.

### 6. Can a self-paced tap-per-word reader work, and what compares?

**Short answer: not as a measure when she is alone, and with no evidence as practice.** A tap has no link to reading, and no trial of a tap-per-word reader for fluency was found. Self-paced word reading is a lab tool ([Just et al. 1982 record](https://scilit.com/publications/8e9032da571a31f7086e1cfc94027cc0), abstract only). The preview-loss worry is probably small for now: slower second graders were largely not disrupted by smaller windows ([Häikiö 2009](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/1826212?lang=en_GB), abstract only), and our text stays on screen.

Tapping through fits the definition of gaming, which is finishing by exploiting the system. In maths tutoring, gaming linked to low prior knowledge rather than to boredom ([Baker 2004](https://hci.rwth-aachen.de/materials/conferences/CHI2004/1p383.pdf)). Moderate; ages 12 to 14, read. So a surge in tap-through may also mean the text is too hard. Supplementary exercises on bypassed material reduced gaming ([Baker 2006](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/11774303_39), abstract only).

All options below run without a backend server. Effort figures are the author's.

**Practical points**
- **Model voice:** Safari may not expose a Lithuanian voice, since downloaded voices have not been available to the page ([WebKit bug 290497](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=290497), read). Check on her iPad. Files generated offline with [Azure lt-LT voices](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=tts) keep the key off the client. A parent's voice should not go in the public repo. No child study compares synthetic with human models.
- **Sync:** word-by-word highlighting did not help learning in French grades 3 to 5 ([Gerbier](https://www.isca-archive.org/slate_2015/gerbier15_slate.pdf), abstract only). Phrase level is enough.
- **Recording stays local to the iPad.** `MediaRecorder` has been safe since iOS 14.5 ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/11648/new-webkit-features-in-safari-14-1/), read). Non-persistent storage can be evicted ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/), read), which is acceptable for disposable clips. Keep the last 5 to 7. Needs a device test, and permission prompts may repeat ([WebKit list](https://lists.webkit.org/pipermail/webkit-unassigned/2024-September/1183557.html), abstract only).
- **Do not upload child audio to Supabase.** The repo is public, and the anon key reaches every browser, so it is effectively public. An anon insert policy would let anyone upload ([Supabase access control](https://supabase.com/docs/guides/storage/security/access-control), read). A child's voice is personal data, and whether the household exemption would hold was not settled ([Recital 18](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-18/)). Not legal advice. Any future change needs a recorded decision.
- **Speech recognition, not now.** The English Reading Tutor showed fluency not significantly different from silent reading in a randomised trial ([Mostow 2013, WWC](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/95240), read). Azure's pronunciation assessment table omits lt-LT ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/ai-services/speech-service/language-support?tabs=pronunciation-assessment), read). Lithuanian child corpora are described as scarce ([Lietuvių kalba](https://journals.vu.lt/lietuviu-kalba/article/download/22492/21756/41329), abstract only), and in-browser Whisper has memory problems on iOS ([WebKit patch](https://github.com/WebKit/JetStream/pull/212.patch), abstract only).
- **Forced pacing, not now.** Fading text has small, mixed evidence, mostly from grade 3 up and mostly silent reading ([Nagler 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00119/full), Weak, read). The closest-age work is old, and it first established each child's maximal rate ([Breznitz 1987](https://cris.haifa.ac.il/en/publications/increasing-first-graders-reading-accuracy-and-comprehension-by-ac/), abstract only). L has no valid start rate.

### Corrections to common citations

- Toste 2020 is a correlational motivation meta-analysis, not a tutoring or intervention study ([record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/A-Meta-Analytic-Review-of-the-Relations/9984383293002771)).
- Therrien 2004 is quoted at .75 and .83 for fluency in different secondary sources, and the original was not read. The 1.70 versus 0.38 criterion contrast comes only via [Kubina 2008](https://centralreach.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/2008-Kubina-Amato-Schwilk-and-Therrien.pdf).
- WWC's null on repeated reading is from grades 5 to 12 ([ASHA summary](https://apps.asha.org/EvidenceMaps/Articles/ArticleSummary/b851e49e-b21d-4af6-96d7-f75cc3a79c5b)), so it says little about age 7.
- Fuchs 1993's 1.5 and 2.0 words per week are grade 2 in most sources, with a thin sample base ([Tindal et al.](https://brtprojects.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/PMGrowthPaperIMRDV9.pdf)). English only.
- Fischer's right-orienting rule is an explanatory model, not a teaching method. No Fischer and Luxembourger training exists in what was found.
- Carnine 1976 tests sounds, not b/d.
- Read Naturally's developer is Candyce Ihnot, not Rasinski.
- Text fading authors are Nagler et al., not "Korbach".
- BRIEF pairs 12 wpm with about 4 s per word. Both hold: about 4 s is that read's median per word, and 12 wpm is a mean of 5 s per word.
- The r of about .67 often quoted for CBM validity was not confirmed.
